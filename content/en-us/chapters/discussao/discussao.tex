\chapter{Discussion}
\label{chap:discussao}
%don't forget to define a unique label to use with the \ref command
We've reached the discussion - the part of the thesis where you try to look analytical and confident, even though you're really just trying to justify why the chart came out looking weird. This is where you take the results and try to extract meaning, context, and impact.

And of course, this is where you pull off that little maneuver to show that, in the end, your work really does have value, even if the charts are a bit crooked, and you wrap it up with something like \enquote{these findings open up space for further investigation.} In other words, \enquote{there's a lot here I couldn't, or didn't have time to, explain, so that's for next time.}
