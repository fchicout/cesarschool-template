\chapter{Methodology}
%don't forget to define a unique label to use with the \ref command
\label{chap:metodologia}

The methodology is the heart of your thesis - or at least it should be. This is where you take everything you did (or tried to do) and describe it as if you had followed a super rational, methodical, carefully thought-out plan. Spoiler: everyone knows that, in many cases, it was more like \enquote{I tried a bunch of things until something worked.}

In this section, don't forget to describe the work in terms of its nature, objective, approach, and procedure.

\section{Research Questions}
\label{sub:questoes_pesquisa}

As aligned with Section \ref{sec:objetivos}:

\begin{itemize}
    \item \textbf{RQ1}: What is the ideal time to wait for a pizza to be ready?
    \item \textbf{RQ2}: What is the ideal temperature for making a pizza?
    \item \textbf{RQ3}: What is the ideal way to make a pizza?
\end{itemize}

\section{Search Strategy}
\label{sub:estrategia_pesquisa}

The search for studies was carried out using the academic databases \textit{\gls{IEEE} Xplore} and \textit{\gls{ACM} Digital Library}, together with the \textit{Google Scholar} search engine. Below is an example search string: \newline

\section{Selection Procedure}
\label{sub:selecao_pesquisa}

The study-selection procedure is generally based on applying inclusion and exclusion criteria. This is where you should demonstrate your scientific rigor.

\subsection{Inclusion Criteria}
\label{sub:criterios_inclusao}

The inclusion criteria were defined as listed below:

\begin{itemize}
    \item The study addresses the ideal way to make pizzas.
    \item The study addresses cooking software.
\end{itemize}

\subsection{Exclusion Criteria}
\label{sub:criterios_exclusao}

The exclusion criteria were defined as listed below:

\begin{itemize}
    \item The study is actually about hamburgers.
    \item It is a duplicate paper.
\end{itemize}
